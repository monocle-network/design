# Open Design

**Open design welcomes the free exchange of ideas, knowledge, processes, and tools. It empowers by lifting barriers to innovation. It opens the door to scaling design projects by distributing adaptable resources.**

## A balance of independence and collaboration

Open-source projects are often driven by global communities. These hubs of learning and building allow individuals to carve their own niche, aligning with projects based on their passions, talents, interests, and beliefs. Work becomes a voluntary public contribution toward common goals.

Open design communities can have multidisciplinary teams with designers, developers, copywriters, translators, and more. These diverse groups develop stronger solutions through varied points of view.

Monocle Network is designed in the open: the application, the markets site, the website, and the wiki are all built collaboratively, and design work is no exception.

## Helpful skills

- Ethics
- Personal responsibility
- Independence & self-motivation
- Written communication
- Open-mindedness
- Respectful feedback
- Time management
- Teamwork
- Openness & transparency

Monocle Network is a text-first project, so clear writing is the most valuable design skill of all: proposals, rationale, and feedback all live in writing.

## Monero has a spirit of openness

It is fair to argue that a network like Monocle needs open design to stay true to its mission.

- Monocle Network is public and open source
- It has public and unrestricted network participation
- It allows permissionless transfer of value

The Haveno software Monocle builds on is itself open source. To a designer, this is a robust foundation for collaboration - one that comes with a responsibility toward the benefit of the people using it.

This responsibility encompasses application design. Most peers interact with the application, not the underlying protocol. It is up to the community to build principled applications.

Access and accessibility go hand in hand. Designing Monocle Network products means removing barriers to entry, and making considered decisions to create a balance of features for ease of use.

## Values

Open-source software development has existed for decades. Public review and debate have helped it create better products. Open design shares similar values, though it is not as widely known or practiced.

### Integrity

- Make moral and responsible choices
- Build with a principled foundation

### Collaboration

- Collaborate without competing
- Approach problems with an open mind
- Share work and process in an open and transparent way
- Make it easy for others to build on the work and ideas
- Be considerate while giving and receiving feedback

### Inclusion

- Aim for inclusiveness, usability, and accessibility
- Design features for the broadest range of people, locations, backgrounds, and levels of technological knowledge
- Embrace multidisciplinary feedback

### Real-world solutions

- Design products that solve real problems and address real needs
- Rely on peers for quality assurance and validation
- Fulfill goals through actions, not intentions
- Create functional elements that have a positive influence on how people feel and think

## Discover design work on Monocle Network

- Join the conversation in the Monocle community spaces (SimpleX, Matrix, and [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/orgs/monocle-network/discussions))
- Review the [design repository](https://github.com/monocle-network/design) - brand assets, guidelines, and current work
- Check the [Help Wanted](https://github.com/monocle-network/meta/issues/2) issue for current needs, including design tasks
- Read the [wiki](https://monocle.wiki) to understand how the network works - good design starts with understanding the thing being designed

### Test, redesign, and engage in feedback

- Explore the application. Download it and, if new to trading, try small amounts. Stagenet is available for risk-free testing
- Redesign an application screen, a website page, or a user flow that interests you. Even as a brief exercise, a redesign is a great way to develop understanding of how something works
- Give feedback on work posted for design review in the community spaces and on GitHub

### Continuous learning

Read, watch, listen, and test. There is a growing body of material on Monero, privacy technology, and peer-to-peer networks. Design material for this space is scarcer - which is exactly why open design contributions are valuable.

### Open up resources

- Try new ways of making knowledge, tools, and workflows public and accessible to others
- Create free and open resources: icon sets, UI kits, research templates, workshops

## Licensing

Open-source licenses ensure freedom of access to information and resources. Their goal is to encourage openness, while protecting creators on attribution and trademark rights. People can use, change, and redistribute work, with the terms varying by license.

The Monocle Network design repository and its contents are released under the [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) license. Anyone is welcome to use, remix, and redistribute the design assets and documentation, provided attribution is given and adaptations are shared under the same license.
